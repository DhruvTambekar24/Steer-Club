# Post-meal walking and blood sugar

**Verdict: QUALIFIED**

**Claim assessed:** “A 10-minute walk after meals can soften the blood-sugar rise.”

**Card copy (28 words):** “A brief walk after a meal may reduce the rise in blood sugar for some people. Ten minutes is promising, but evidence is limited and results can vary.”

## Evidence

- A 2025 randomized crossover study in 12 healthy young adults found lower two-hour glucose exposure and lower peak glucose after a comfortable 10-minute treadmill walk immediately after a 75 g glucose drink, compared with sitting. This directly tests a 10-minute dose, but a glucose drink is not an ordinary meal and the small, selected sample limits generalization. [1]
- A 2023 systematic review/meta-analysis of eight crossover trials (116 participants; 47 with type 2 diabetes) found lower post-meal glucose excursions with post-meal exercise than with inactivity or pre-meal exercise. The authors noted high risk of bias; the review supports the general direction, not a universal 10-minute dose. [2]

**Caveat:** Short post-meal walking may blunt post-meal glucose for some people, but it is not guaranteed, does not establish prevention of diabetes or other disease, and the exact 10-minute claim rests on limited evidence. Educational information only, not personal medical advice.
