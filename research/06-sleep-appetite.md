# Sleep and next-day appetite

**Claim:** Poor sleep can make you hungrier the next day.

**Verdict: QUALIFIED.** Experimental evidence supports a possible effect, but not a universal one. A systematic review of randomized trials found that several sleep-restriction studies reported higher food intake, and one reported greater hunger after a night without sleep. The review cautioned that food-intake methods varied and the studies were brief; effects on appetite-related hormones were inconsistent. [1]

A separate small, non-randomized at-home study of 15 young adults with overweight found greater hunger and food intake the day after a night of moderate sleep restriction. Its small, specific sample limits how broadly the result applies. [2]

**Card copy (22 words):** A short night may leave some people feeling hungrier or eating more the next day. Effects vary, and not everyone notices a change.

**Caveat:** This evidence concerns short-term sleep restriction, not every kind of poor sleep or every person. It supports “can” or “may,” not an inevitable next-day effect.

## References

[1]: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4532553/ "A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials of the Impact of Sleep Duration on Adiposity and Components of Energy Balance"
[2]: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9991999/ "A single night of moderate sleep restriction at-home increases hunger and food intake in overweight young adults"
