# Evidence note: dinner skipping and a “metabolism reset”

**Claim:** “Skipping dinner resets your metabolism.”

**Verdict:** **MYTH** — evidence does not support a metabolic “reset” from skipping dinner. Do not infer that everyone needs regular meals.

## Evidence

- Nas et al., “Impact of breakfast skipping compared with dinner skipping on regulation of energy balance and metabolic risk,” *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition* (2017): randomized crossover, controlled-feeding study in 17 adults compared a three-meal day with single breakfast- or dinner-skipping days. Under these short, isocaloric chamber conditions, 24-hour energy expenditure was higher on both skip days than control. This does not show a reset or a lasting change; it shows that one skipped dinner did not lower measured daily expenditure in this small acute study. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0002916522049085
- Ravussin et al., “Early Time-Restricted Feeding Reduces Appetite and Increases Fat Oxidation but Does Not Affect Energy Expenditure in Humans,” *Obesity* (2019): randomized crossover feeding study tested an early six-hour eating window (08:00–14:00) against a 12-hour window. The early window did not significantly change 24-hour energy expenditure or resting energy expenditure, despite changes in fuel use and appetite. It is a short, small study of early time-restricted eating, not proof about every dinner-skipping pattern. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6658129/

## Suggested card copy

**MYTH:** Skipping dinner does not “reset” metabolism. Small controlled studies found short-term changes in energy use, not evidence of a lasting metabolic reset.

**Caveat:** Direct dinner-skipping evidence is limited and short-term; meal timing can affect short-term fuel use and metabolic measures. These studies do not establish a lasting reset. Educational copy, not personal medical advice.
