# Evidence note: whole fruit and fruit juice

**Claim:** “Fruit juice is the same as whole fruit.”

**Verdict:** MYTH. Juice can provide fruit, but it is not nutritionally or practically identical to eating whole fruit.

**Card copy (34 words):** Myth: Juice isn’t the same as whole fruit: it usually has less fiber and is easier to drink in larger portions. It can affect blood glucose differently, but whole fruit remains a healthy choice.

Whole fruit retains more fiber and takes chewing; juice is quick to drink, so larger amounts can be consumed more easily. The NHS counts only 150 ml of juice or smoothie as one daily fruit-and-vegetable portion, while 80 g of whole fruit counts as one portion. WHO says fruit juice, including unsweetened varieties, contains significant free sugars and recommends fruit as a carbohydrate source. Evidence on glucose responses is not a basis for claiming juice always causes a larger rise: results depend on the fruit, form, and study, and should be described cautiously.

## Sources

1. WHO, [Healthy diet](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet) — supports choosing fruit among primary carbohydrate sources, adequate dietary fibre, and limiting free sugars, including sugars naturally present in fruit juice.
2. NHS, [5 A Day: what counts?](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/eat-well/5-a-day/5-a-day-what-counts/) — supports the practical portion distinction: 80 g whole fruit is one portion; juice/smoothie is limited to 150 ml and counts as one portion only.
3. Mavadiya et al., [Whole Fruits Versus 100% Fruit Juice: Revisiting the Evidence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12398644/) — peer-reviewed review discussing higher fiber and satiety with whole fruit, lower fiber in juice, and evidence on glucose/metabolic responses; supports careful, non-absolute wording.

**Caveat:** Glucose responses vary with fruit, preparation, amount, and individual. Do not suggest that all juice causes a spike or that people should avoid fruit. This is general educational copy, not personal medical advice.
