# Claim 10: Intermittent fasting is necessary for metabolic health

**Verdict: MYTH** — Intermittent fasting is a dietary pattern that may help some people, but available evidence does not establish it as necessary for metabolic health. A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis of 20 randomized trials found intermittent fasting may be an alternative to calorie restriction, but was not superior overall; long-term evidence was limited.[1] A peer-reviewed review likewise describes possible metabolic benefits while noting comparable adherence and outcomes versus continuous restriction, with individual responses varying.[2] An NIH summary of one 3-month trial found modest benefits in adults with metabolic syndrome, but says longer studies are needed; this shows potential in a studied group, not a universal need.[3]

**Card copy (31 words):** Intermittent fasting is one way of timing meals, not a requirement for metabolic health. Studies show possible benefits for some people, but it has not proved superior to other eating approaches.

**Caveat:** Evidence, especially on long-term outcomes, is still developing. People with relevant health conditions should discuss fasting with a clinician. This is educational information, not personal medical advice.
